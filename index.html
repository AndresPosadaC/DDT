Dissolve · Design · Tell

An integrated transformation framework: help leaders rethink complex, multi-actor problems by redesigning systems

Andrés Posada · Framework v0.3 · 2026

FIND THE PURPOSE · WHY

Dissolve, don’t patch.

We redesign the system behind the problem so every actor gains, and turn evidence into decisions that last.

Tap an icon on the outer ring, or a step on the inner ring, to see what each step delivers. The journey runs clockwise, like a hero’s journey.
STEP 1 · YOU · CREATIVE MIND

Engage SwD + DP

Who decides, and what decision is at stake?

DELIVERABLES
  • Engagement brief
  • Decision & sponsor map
  • Steering committee
  • Draft Big Idea: elevator pitch
KEY ACTIVITIES

Identify the decision-maker and the decision; form the steering committee; agree tone, format and timing; draft the Big Idea as a hypothesis.
STEP 2 · NEED · CREATIVE MIND

Understand DP + VPD

See the mess through every actor’s eyes.

DELIVERABLES
  • ◆ As-Is process & data diagnosis
  • Actor profiles: jobs, pains, gains
  • System map + rich text
KEY ACTIVITIES

Map every relevant actor, including the unrepresented; interview, run workshops, observe; describe the system with time series, scatterplots, fishbone, causal-loop diagrams, an incidence matrix and process flowcharts.
STEP 3 · GO · ANALYTICAL MIND

Frame DP + SwD

Commit to the problem worth dissolving.

DELIVERABLES
  • Relevant-problem statement
  • Reference projection chart
  • Controllability matrix
  • 3-minute story
KEY ACTIVITIES

Identify critical nodes and candidate causes; check where actors’ pains cluster; project the do-nothing future; assess who can act on each variable.
STEP 4 · SEARCH · ANALYTICAL MIND

Design DP + VPD

Specify the ideal system and its value.

DELIVERABLES
  • ◆ To-Be process redesign
  • Idealized system (TASCOI)
  • Value map per actor
  • Future narrative
KEY ACTIVITIES

Generate driving ideas and alternatives; specify how the system operates; map value for each actor; check that it is feasible, viable and able to improve.
STEP 5 · FIND · ANALYTICAL MIND

Mitigate VPD + DP

Test the riskiest bets; find what works.

DELIVERABLES
  • ◆ Up to 5 tech options rated by effort, impact, risk
  • Obstacle chain
  • Hypothesis board + pilot results
KEY ACTIVITIES

◆ Identify and test assumptions before committing resources; chain obstacles across rules, actors and results; run cheap tests, from what people say to what they do.
STEP 6 · TAKE · ANALYTICAL MIND

Plan DP

Pay the price: obstacles become projects.

DELIVERABLES
  • ◆ Data & AI roadmap
  • Logframe per project
  • KPIs & means of verification
  • Key assumptions
  • Budget & timeline
KEY ACTIVITIES

Transform critical obstacles into fundable projects; write objectives with direct verbs; name an owner for each project; plan for the assumptions that could sink it.
STEP 7 · RETURN · CREATIVE MIND

Decide SwD

Bring the story back to decision-makers.

DELIVERABLES
  • Decision deck or memo
  • Storyboard + clean visuals
  • A clear call to action
KEY ACTIVITIES

Revisit the context; finalize the recommendation; storyboard the narrative; choose the flow; build the visuals; check clarity; align stakeholders before the meeting.
STEP 8 · CHANGE · CREATIVE MIND

Run & learn DP + VPD + SwD

Transformed, and still learning.

DELIVERABLES
  • ◆ Data foundation live
  • Dashboards + learning cards
  • ◆ Data-culture program
  • ◆ 2–3 certified champions
KEY ACTIVITIES

Monitor indicators and assumptions; capture learning cards; evaluate in single and double loops; tell the story of progress.
CASE · CARTAGENA, COLOMBIA

From “which cars?” to “which match?”

Five partners want to buy cars (USD 40–60k each), hire top drivers and offer VIP rides to executives, tourists, medical patients and event visitors. They ask AI: which cars, and how many, should we buy to run on Uber?

Follow the journey and watch the purpose in the center change. Figures are illustrative assumptions, not a forecast.
CASE · STEP 1 · YOU

Engage

Why now: “Earn a second income with good cars.”

WHAT HAPPENS

Five partners, USD 200–300k of personal capital, and a ready answer: buy cars. The first real decision is governance: one lead partner plus a majority vote.

KEY OUTPUT
  • Engagement brief
  • Draft Big Idea: “Premium only works if clients pay for a better experience. Prove that first.”
CASE · STEP 2 · NEED

Understand

Why now: still “good cars”, but the mess appears.

WHAT HAPPENS

Four segments: executives, tourists (about 5.5M passengers through Nov 2025), medical patients (Hospital Serena del Mar is the natural anchor), and events (Andicom 2026 drew 6,000+ attendees). Maintenance, taxes, insurance and client acquisition are undefined. Bill 347 of 2026, which threatened private-car apps, was withdrawn in February, but those apps still lack a clear legal framework: depending on Uber is a liability.

KEY OUTPUT
  • ◆ As-Is diagnosis
  • Profiles per segment: jobs, pains, gains
  • Map of hotels, clinics, event organizers, regulator
CASE · STEP 3 · GO

Frame

Why now: “Win and keep the right clients.”

Doing nothing different means buying 5 cars at USD 50k and running them on an app: USD 250k at risk. At 12 premium trips a day, cash is still −USD 157k after 3 years; selling the cars brings it to about −USD 3k, a break-even at best. The bet rides on a number the partners don’t control, in a channel that is legally contested. Clients already pay a 2–4× premium for executive transfers (about USD 39–77 vs. COP 60,000 standard).
CASE · STEP 4 · SEARCH

Design

Driving idea: the driver is the product; cars are a leasable commodity.

WHAT HAPPENS

The business matches premium clients with drivers whose skills fit them: languages, local history, medical-escort care, event logistics. It sells mainly through its own channel (hotels, clinics, event organizers, corporate accounts), operating legally as special transport, with Uber only as a backup.

KEY OUTPUT
  • ◆ To-Be model: own channel first
  • TASCOI of the matching system
  • Value map per segment
CASE · STEP 5 · FIND

Mitigate

Test before you buy: a cheap pilot comes before any car or app.

THE TESTS
  • 20 clients, 6 weeks: right if at least 40% rebook a matched driver
  • Landing page: “luxury car” vs. “your bilingual driver”
  • Pitch Serena del Mar’s international-patient office and 4 hotels: right if at least 2 sign
  • Legal check with a transport lawyer
KEY OUTPUT
  • ◆ 5 tech options; chosen: WhatsApp + CRM concierge, plus B2B referrals
  • Obstacle chain
  • Hypothesis board
CASE · STEP 6 · TAKE

Plan

Pay the price: the partners let go of “we wanted cars”.

THE HYBRID NUMBERS

USD 20k to launch with leased cars; the app is built only after the pilot. Maximum exposure about USD 56k, break-even in month 26, +USD 99k by month 36. If own-channel revenue is 30% lower: −USD 107k. That downside is why the pilot gate exists.

KEY OUTPUT
  • Logframe: 100 recurring clients in 12 months
  • ◆ Data & AI roadmap
  • Budget and pilot gate
CASE · STEP 7 · RETURN

Decide

Why now: “Match people, create journeys.”

THE BIG IDEA

“Don’t buy cars yet. Fund a 3-month concierge pilot (about USD 20k) that proves Cartagena’s premium clients will rebook a matched driver. If at least 40% rebook, lease the fleet and build the matching app.”

Call to action: approve the pilot, name the lead partner, commission the legal check.
CASE · STEP 8 · CHANGE

Run & learn

The new why: trusted, exclusive journeys with the driver who fits each client.

WHAT HAPPENS

AI moves to the right place: a matching engine (client needs ↔ driver skills) and demand forecasting around events and cruise seasons. The double loop asks: is this a ride business, or a driver-talent and hospitality agency?

KEY OUTPUT
  • ◆ Data foundation from day one
  • Dashboard: rebooking, satisfaction by match, revenue per driver-hour
  • Learning cards

How to read it
Start at the center: purpose, why this matters. The middle ring is how we address it, eight phases moving clockwise, like a hero’s journey. The colored half (steps 1, 2, 7 and 8) is the creative, human mind: empathy, imagination, story. The gray half (steps 3 to 6) is the analytical mind: frame, model, test, plan. The outer ring holds what each step delivers.
Three books, one line
DP Dissolving Problems: frame the mess, design the ideal, turn obstacles into projects.
VPD Value Proposition Design: find what each actor truly needs, and test it before building.
SwD Storytelling with Data: context, clarity and a call to action.
◆ Digital transformation track
Diamonds mark the digital-transformation deliverables: As-Is diagnosis → To-Be redesign → up to five technology options → data & AI roadmap → data foundation, data culture and certified champions.
