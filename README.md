# DDT
A new consulting line, “Dissolve · Design · Tell,” integrates systemic problem-solving, customer-centered solutions, and effective communication. The method, presented as a spiral with seven phases, emphasizes dissolving problems by redesigning systems, ensuring value for all actors, and using evidence-based storytelling to drive decisions.

# Dissolve · Design · Tell
## An Integrated Consulting Line from Three Books

**Working title options:** *Dissolve · Design · Tell* (EN) / *Disolver · Diseñar · Contar* (ES)
**Author:** Andrés Posada (draft v0.2, October 2026: adds the Golden Circle × Story Circle view, the digital transformation track, and template T1)
**Source books:**
- **DP:** Aldana & Reyes, *Disolver Problemas: Criterio para Formular Proyectos Sociales* (Uniandes)
- **VPD:** Osterwalder, Pigneur, Bernarda & Smith, *Value Proposition Design* (Wiley, 2014)
- **SwD:** Knaflic, *Storytelling with Data* (Wiley, 2015)

> **Status: first draft for refinement.** Everything here is a proposal to react to. Each section ends with points to decide.

---

## 1. Why Combine These Three Books

Each book answers a different question that every engagement has to answer:

| Question | Book | What it brings | What it lacks alone |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What problem are we really solving, and for whom?** | **DP** | Systemic framing of messy, multi-actor problems. Redesigning the system instead of patching symptoms. Obstacle analysis and the logical framework. | A sharp idea of the *value* each actor receives. Little about communication. Its testing is mostly monitoring after the fact. |
| **What should we offer so that people actually want it?** | **VPD** | Customer-centered fit (jobs, pains, gains). Many prototypes. Cheap, evidence-based tests before building. | Assumes a market and a customer segment. Weak on institutions, politics, and many stakeholders with conflicting interests. |
| **How do we get decision-makers to understand and act?** | **SwD** | Audience-first communication, the Big Idea, clean visuals, and a narrative arc with a call to action | Assumes the analysis and the recommendation already exist |

**The combined promise:** *we dissolve the problem by redesigning the system (DP), make sure every actor gets real value from the redesign (VPD), and turn the evidence into decisions (SwD).*

**Positioning statement** (VPD's ad-lib format, applied to the consulting line itself):
> Our consulting helps **leaders of public, private, and mixed organizations** who want to **solve complex, multi-actor problems** by **avoiding reactive fixes that backfire and proposals that nobody adopts**, and by **giving them a validated design, a fundable plan, and a story that moves decision-makers**.

*To decide:* the final name; whether the line is marketed in Spanish, in English, or in both.

## 2. Guiding Principles

1. **Dissolve before you solve.** Ask what system produces the problem before asking how to fix it. *(DP)*
2. **Problems live in relationships.** Observe the whole system, not just its parts, and beware of fixes that backfire. *(DP)*
3. **Start from the people, not the solution.** For each actor: what jobs, pains, and gains does this system have for them? *(VPD + DP)*
4. **Design the future first, then remove the obstacles.** A desired destination beats a list of complaints. *(DP)*
5. **Every proposal is a set of hypotheses.** Test the riskiest one first, and cheaply. *(VPD; DP's risk chain)*
6. **Evidence over opinion, behavior over stated intentions.** *(VPD, DP)*
7. **Show the pearls, not the oysters.** Communicate only what serves the decision. *(SwD)*
8. **Every deliverable ends in a decision or an action.** *(SwD's call to action; DP's logframe)*
9. **Learn in double loops.** Question not only *how well* we did, but *whether this was the right way*. *(DP, VPD's Evolve)*
10. **Good solutions are ethical and beautiful.** If the solution doesn't inspire the people involved, it won't be adopted. *(DP)*

## 3. The Method at a Glance

Seven phases. As in DP, they are **moments in a spiral, not a waterfall**: phases 1–4 usually take two or three loops. **Storytelling runs through every phase**, not only at the end.

```
 0. ENGAGE ──► 1. UNDERSTAND ──► 2. FRAME ──► 3. DESIGN ──► 4. DE-RISK ──► 5. PLAN ──► 6. DECIDE ──► 7. RUN & LEARN
                    ▲                                            │                                         │
                    └──────────── spiral: revise the mess ◄──────┘◄──────────── double-loop learning ◄─────┘
   ─────────────── TELL (SwD): Big Idea · storyboard · visuals · call to action at every phase ───────────────
```

| # | Phase | Key question | Lead book | Main deliverable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Engage** | Who decides, and what decision do they need to make? | SwD + DP | Engagement brief |
| 1 | **Understand** | What is the mess, from each actor's point of view? | DP + VPD | Rich text + stakeholder profiles |
| 2 | **Frame** | Which problem, if dissolved, changes the most, and who can act on it? | DP | Relevant-problem statement + reference projection |
| 3 | **Design** | What system would dissolve it, and what value does it give each actor? | DP + VPD | Idealized design + value maps |
| 4 | **De-risk** | What could stop it, and which assumptions are riskiest? | DP + VPD | Obstacle chain + hypothesis board + test results |
| 5 | **Plan** | What projects turn the design into reality, and how will we know they're working? | DP | Logical framework(s) |
| 6 | **Decide** | What should the decision-maker do, and why now? | SwD | Decision deck or memo |
| 7 | **Run & learn** | Is it working? Is this still the right path? | DP + VPD + SwD | Monitoring dashboard + learning cards |

### 3.1 The Framework as Golden Circle × Story Circle (the infographic view)

The framework can also be presented as **three concentric rings** (Sinek's *Start with Why*). The middle ring is traveled as a **hero's journey**, using Dan Harmon's 8-step Story Circle. **The client is the hero, and the consultant is the mentor.**

| Ring | Content |
|---|---|
| **WHY** (center) | *Dissolve, don't patch.* We redesign the system behind the problem so every actor gains, and turn evidence into decisions that last. |
| **HOW** (middle) | The 8 phases, clockwise, as the Story Circle (below) |
| **WHAT** (outer) | The tangible deliverables of each phase. ◆ marks the digital-transformation items. |

| Story Circle step | Phase | The client's journey |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · **YOU**: a character in a zone of comfort | 0 Engage | A leader in a known world, with a decision to make |
| 2 · **NEED**: but they want something | 1 Understand | Something isn't working. We see the mess through every actor's eyes. |
| 3 · **GO**: they enter an unfamiliar situation | 2 Frame | Crossing the threshold: committing to the problem worth dissolving |
| 4 · **SEARCH**: they adapt to it | 3 Design | Imagining the ideal system and the value it brings to each actor |
| 5 · **FIND**: they get what they wanted | 4 De-risk | Testing the riskiest bets and finding what really works |
| 6 · **TAKE**: they pay a heavy price | 5 Plan | Facing the obstacles; committing budget, time, and political capital |
| 7 · **RETURN**: they go back to the familiar | 6 Decide | Bringing the story back to the decision-makers |
| 8 · **CHANGE**: having changed | 7 Run & learn | A transformed organization that keeps learning |

**Two modes of thinking.** The How ring is split down the middle, a vertical version of Harmon's known world and unknown world:
- **Creative mind (colored, right half): steps 7, 8, 1, 2** (Decide, Run & learn, Engage, Understand). Empathy, listening, imagination, and story: the human side of the work.
- **Analytical mind (gray, left half): steps 3, 4, 5, 6** (Frame, Design, De-risk, Plan). Framing, modeling, testing, and planning: the structured side.

The client crosses into the analytical half at step 3 (*Go*), when they commit to dissolving the problem instead of patching it. They return to the creative half at step 7 (*Return*) to tell the story and lead the change. The ring segments are drawn as interlocking arrows, so the journey reads clockwise. Step 1 starts just below the 3 o'clock position, which gives the two halves a clean vertical split.

*Note:* the "left brain / right brain" image is a teaching **metaphor** for two modes of thinking. Neuroscience doesn't support a strict split between the hemispheres.

## 4. The Phases in Detail

### Phase 0 — Engage: frame the commission
**Purpose:** agree on who decides, what decision is at stake, and who must take part.

| Activity | Tool | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Identify the **decision-maker** and the decision they must make | Who / What / How | SwD Ch. 1; DP Ch. II |
| Form the **Steering Committee** with the decision-maker and key actors | Steering Committee (*Comité Guía*) | DP Ch. II |
| Agree on the tone, the mechanism (live or written), and the timing of the final decision | Context questions | SwD Ch. 1 |
| Write a **draft Big Idea** as a hypothesis ("we believe the real issue is…") | Big Idea (provisional) | SwD Ch. 1 |

**Deliverable: a one-page engagement brief.** It covers the decision-maker, the decision, the committee, the scope, a draft Big Idea, and the timeline.
**Quality check:** can you say in one sentence what decision the work will enable, and for whom?

---

### Phase 1 — Understand: the mess, through everyone's eyes
**Purpose:** a shared, systemic understanding of the situation from the viewpoints of all relevant actors.

| Activity | Tool | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Map the relevant actors, including those harmed, those who benefit, regulators, resource holders, and **unrepresented** parties | 6-question actor heuristic | DP Ch. II |
| Gather perceptions | Interviews, surveys, workshops, focus groups, field diary, documents | DP Ch. II; VPD research roles (journalist, anthropologist, data detective) |
| **Build one profile per key actor** | Customer Profile: jobs / pains / gains, ranked | VPD Canvas |
| Describe the system | Time series, scatterplots, fishbone (with caution), **causal-loop diagrams**, **incidence matrix**, process flowcharts | DP Ch. II |
| Explore the data, and keep exploration separate from what you'll show | Exploratory analysis | SwD |
| Consolidate the understanding, including the disagreements | **Rich text** (5–7k words) | DP Ch. II |

**Integration rule.** In DP the "actors" are people with interests in the system. **Each one can be profiled as a VPD customer.** Their *pains* make up the problematic situation, their *gains* point to the ideal, and their *jobs* show what the system must enable. This gives VPD's B2B stakeholder idea the depth of DP.

**Deliverables:**
- the rich text;
- a stakeholder map;
- one actor profile for each key actor;
- one causal-loop diagram;
- one incidence matrix.

**Quality check:** would each actor recognize their own viewpoint in the rich text? Did a new round of interviews stop adding anything new?

---

### Phase 2 — Frame: choose the relevant problem
**Purpose:** move from "everything is connected" to one or a few **critical nodes** that are worth acting on and can actually be acted on.

| Activity | Tool | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Find candidate causes with many links to other variables | Incidence matrix + causal loops + fishbone | DP Ch. II |
| Check where actors' highest-ranked pains cluster | Ranked pains across actor profiles | VPD |
| Project the future if nothing is done (10–15 years) | **Reference projection** | DP Ch. II |
| Check who can act on each variable | **Controllability matrix** (C/I) | DP Ch. II |
| Write the diagnosis | **Big Idea + 3-minute story** | SwD Ch. 1 |
| Show it | One line chart: history + "do nothing" projection, annotated | SwD Ch. 2, 4, 5 |

**Relevant-problem test** (DP). The problem should be:
- an important cause;
- linked to other causes;
- bigger in future impact;
- **controllable by someone at the table**.

**Deliverable: the problem framing note.** It contains the relevant problem, the reference projection chart, the controllability map, and the Big Idea of the diagnosis.

**First decision checkpoint with the Steering Committee:** *"Do we agree this is the problem worth dissolving?"*

---

### Phase 3 — Design: the ideal system and the value it delivers
**Purpose:** describe a desired, feasible system from which the problem disappears, and make sure it creates real value for each actor.

| Activity | Tool | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Generate driving ideas: *"what would have to happen for this problem to disappear?"* Then choose the less obvious answers. | Idea-fuerza; supply vs. demand and other metaphors; brainstorming; Six Thinking Hats; analogies | DP Ch. III, Annex A |
| Create **several alternatives**, not one | Prototyping: napkin sketches, ad-libs | VPD Design |
| Narrate the future so that stakeholders can see it | **Futuristic narrative or scenarios** | DP Ch. III; SwD "what could be" |
| Specify the system operationally | **"System S does X through Y for Z"** + **TASCOI** + conceptual model (verbs, precedence) + structural models | DP Ch. III |
| **Map the value for each actor** | **Value Map**: pain relievers and gain creators against each actor's profile | VPD Canvas |
| Check the design | Technologically feasible, operationally viable, capable of improvement + **problem–solution fit** for every key actor | DP Ch. III; VPD |

**Integration rule.** DP's idealized design is the *system*. VPD's value maps are *what each actor gets from that system*.
- **If any key actor has no fit, the design will meet resistance.** That actor then becomes an obstacle in Phase 4.
- **TASCOI's roles** (clients, suppliers, actors, owners, interveners) **are a good list of the value propositions you need.**

**Deliverables:**
- the idealized design document: system name, TASCOI, conceptual model;
- a value map for each key actor;
- a 1–2 page future narrative;
- a storyboard of how the change will be explained.

**Quality check:** does the design make the critical node disappear, or only shrink it? Would the actors find it *attractive*, not merely acceptable?

---

### Phase 4 — De-risk: obstacles and riskiest assumptions
**Purpose:** find out what could stop the design and test the most dangerous assumptions before committing resources.

| Activity | Tool | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Identify obstacles | Cultural, political, legal; past partial solutions | DP Ch. IV |
| Explain and chain them | **Institutions → actors → results** (rules of the game, players, outcomes) | DP Ch. IV |
| Turn the design into explicit hypotheses | "Every value proposition is a set of hypotheses" | VPD Test |
| Prioritize hypotheses by **importance × lack of evidence** | Hypothesis board | VPD Test |
| Test cheaply, from weak evidence (what people say) to strong evidence (what they do and pay for) | **Test Cards / Learning Cards**; experiments (landing page, mock sale, concierge or Wizard of Oz, pilot, "buy a feature") | VPD Test |
| Show the evidence | Before-and-after charts, slopegraphs, simple text for key numbers | SwD Ch. 2 |
| Select the **critical obstacles** | Chained obstacle diagram | DP Ch. IV |

**Integration rule.** DP's obstacles cover the **systemic and institutional risks** (rules, power, politics). VPD's hypotheses cover **adoption and value risks** (will people use it, want it, pay for it). **A complete risk picture needs both.**
- In public or social engagements, many VPD tests become **pilots, workshops with real users, or co-design sessions** instead of market tests.

**Deliverables:**
- the obstacle chain;
- the hypothesis board;
- completed test cards and learning cards;
- a revised idealized design, if the evidence says so. This is the spiral back to Phases 1–3.

**Second decision checkpoint:** *"Here is what we learned. Do we proceed, pivot, or stop?"*

---

### Phase 5 — Plan: projects that remove the obstacles
**Purpose:** turn each critical obstacle into a fundable, measurable project.

| Activity | Tool | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Write the logical chain: **finality** (from the idealized design) → **purpose** (the obstacle removed) → **products** → **activities** | Logical framework, using **direct verbs** (reduce by…, increase by…), not sinuous ones (support, coordinate…) | DP Ch. V |
| Make the assumptions explicit at every level, and handle the lethal ones | Risk chain + vulnerability analysis | DP Ch. V |
| Define indicators: practical, relevant, focused, verifiable (quantity, quality, time) + means of verification | Control chain | DP Ch. V |
| Link to the earlier tests | Test-card metrics become the first indicators | VPD |
| Check the business or financing model | Business model fit; who pays and who sustains it | VPD |
| Name an **owner** for each project | *Dueño* or *doliente* | DP Ch. V |

**Deliverables:**
- one logframe per project;
- a project portfolio map showing which project removes which obstacle;
- a high-level budget and timeline.

**Quality check:**
- Horizontal logic of the logframe: does each level truly cause the next?
- Is every indicator *relevant*, that is, attributable to this project only?

---

### Phase 6 — Decide: the story that moves the decision-maker
**Purpose:** get a clear decision. *Approval* is not the goal; *action* is.

| Activity | Tool | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Revisit the context with the decision-maker in mind | Who / What / How | SwD Ch. 1 |
| Finalize the **Big Idea** of the recommendation | Point of view + what's at stake + one sentence | SwD Ch. 1 |
| Storyboard the narrative | **Beginning:** the mess and what's at stake (reference projection). **Middle:** the designed future, the value for each actor, the evidence, the options. **End:** the projects and the call to action. | SwD Ch. 7; DP |
| Choose the flow | Chronological, to build credibility, or **lead with the ending**, for executives | SwD Ch. 7 |
| Build the visuals | Right chart, decluttered, focused attention, action titles, annotations | SwD Ch. 2–5 |
| Check clarity | Horizontal and vertical logic, reverse storyboarding, a fresh pair of eyes | SwD Ch. 7 |
| Align stakeholders before the meeting | Share the storyboard early; win over a vocal ally | SwD Ch. 1, 5 |

**Suggested story arc for the decision deck:**
1. **Setting:** the situation as each key actor experiences it.
2. **Conflict:** the reference projection, or what happens if we do nothing.
3. **The turn:** the driving idea, a different way of seeing the problem.
4. **The future:** the idealized design and what each actor gains.
5. **Proof:** test results and evidence.
6. **The path:** the obstacles and the projects (logframe summary).
7. **Call to action:** the decision we need today, from whom, and by when.

**Optional enrichments:** Aristotle's ethos / pathos / logos, Sinek's *Start with Why*, and Harmon's story circle all fit naturally on this arc.

**Deliverables:** the decision deck (live) and/or the decision memo (written). Don't build a single "slideument" to do both jobs.

---

### Phase 7 — Run & learn
**Purpose:** carry out the plan, monitor it, and keep learning whether the path is still right.

| Activity | Tool | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Monitor activities and products against the indicators | Control chain + means of verification | DP Ch. V |
| Watch the assumptions and the uncontrollable variables (disturbances) | Risk chain; controllability matrix | DP Ch. II, V |
| Capture learning | Learning Cards ("we believed… we observed… we learned… therefore…") | VPD |
| Evaluate. Single loop: are we doing it right? Double loop: is this the right way to reach the finality? | Learning cycle | DP Ch. V; VPD Evolve |
| Report progress | Decluttered dashboard with action titles; **the story of progress** | SwD |
| Restart the spiral when the context changes | Revised mess → revised design | DP Ch. IV |

**Deliverables:** a monitoring dashboard, periodic learning reviews, and an updated design.

### 4.1 The Digital Transformation Track (◆)

When an engagement involves processes, data, automation, or AI, a **digital transformation track** runs inside the same phases. It generalizes the approach used in recent proposals (process diagnosis, digitization and automation with AI) so that it can apply to any client.

| Phase | ◆ Digital transformation deliverable | Key rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Understand | **As-Is diagnosis** of processes, data, systems, and capabilities (bottlenecks, manual steps, data silos and quality, legacy systems) | Map the *process* before the technology |
| 3 Design | **To-Be redesign**: the target process and operating model, with the idealized system for the data and the people | **Redesign the process first. Never automate a bad process.** |
| 4 De-risk | **At most 5 technology options**, each rated by **effort, impact, and risk**, plus a proof of concept or pilot for the preferred one | Present options *before* deciding. Avoid a single vendor-led answer. |
| 5 Plan | **Data & AI roadmap**: the technical foundation (integration, ETL/ELT, a central data platform, governance) and a sequenced portfolio of AI and automation use cases | Use cases only on top of trusted data |
| 6 Decide | A decision session built with **storytelling with data** | One Big Idea; options compared visually |
| 7 Run & learn | **The data foundation in operation**, a **data-culture program** (training, dashboards, champions), and **2–3 key people certified** on the tools as a milestone | Adoption is the deliverable, not just the system |

**Typical risks to name up front**, and the phase where each is handled:
- **Resistance to cultural change:** phases 1 and 7.
- **Data silos and poor data quality in legacy systems:** phases 1 and 5.
- **Scope creep:** phase 0 and a change-control process.
- **Bureaucracy and public governance:** phase 4's institutional obstacles.

## 7. Adapting the Line to Engagement Types

| Engagement type | Lead emphasis | Typical adjustments |
|---|---|---|
| **Public / social** (ministries, municipalities, development agencies) | **DP-led** | Full actor mapping, including unrepresented parties. Institutional obstacle analysis is essential. Tests become pilots and co-design workshops. The logframe is often required by funders. |
| **Private product or service** (companies, startups) | **VPD-led** | Customer segments instead of "actors." Market tests and business model fit. DP's systemic view guards against offers that backfire in the wider ecosystem. |
| **Mixed / data and AI transformation** (agencies or companies adopting analytics or AI) | **Balanced** | DP for the organizational mess and institutional obstacles. VPD for the value to each user group (end users, managers, IT, regulators). SwD for the dashboards and the decision story. |
| **Pure analytical or reporting work** | **SwD-led** | Phase 0 context + Phase 6 story. DP's reference projection and VPD's audience profile sharpen the "so what." |

## 8. Worked Mini-Example (Illustrative)

*A public agency wants to "implement AI" but has low adoption of its existing data systems.*

| Phase | What the integrated line produces |
|---|---|
| 0 | The decision-maker is the agency director. Decision: approve an 18-month AI and data roadmap. |
| 1 | Actors: analysts, middle managers, IT, citizens, the oversight body, the vendor. Analysts' **pains**: duplicate data entry and distrust of the data. Managers' **gains**: faster reporting. Causal loop: low data quality → low use → less investment in data quality. |
| 2 | **Relevant problem:** no one owns data quality. That is controllable by the director. **Reference projection:** AI pilots trained on poor data fail, and trust falls further. |
| 3 | **Driving idea:** *stop asking "where can we use AI" and ask "what would make people trust the data?"* **Idealized design:** a data-stewardship system ("the agency certifies its key data, through named stewards and automated checks, so that decisions and AI rest on trusted information"). TASCOI defined. A value map for each actor. |
| 4 | Obstacles: an informal rule that "data is IT's job," no budget line, vendor lock-in. Riskiest hypothesis: *analysts will spend 10% of their time on stewardship.* Test: a 6-week concierge pilot in one unit. |
| 5 | Two logframes: (a) a data-stewardship program; (b) a first AI use case built on certified data. |
| 6 | Big Idea: *"Our AI will only be as good as our data. Investing first in data ownership cuts the risk of failed AI pilots and gives the director reliable reports within six months."* Decision deck with a lead-with-the-ending flow. |
| 7 | A dashboard of certified datasets and use; quarterly learning cards; a double-loop review at month 9. |

## 9. Teaching Adaptation (Draft)

**Possible format:** 8 sessions of 3 hours, for an MBA elective or a continuing-education course. Each session has a short theory block, a tool practice, and work on a **team case that runs through the whole course**.

| Session | Theme | Tools practiced | Case milestone |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Dissolve, don't solve: systems and messes | Solve/resolve/dissolve; counterintuitive consequences | Choose the case; engagement brief |
| 2 | Seeing through others' eyes | Actor heuristic, interviews, actor profiles (VPD) | Stakeholder map + profiles |
| 3 | Describing the system | Causal loops, incidence matrix, exploratory data | Rich-text draft |
| 4 | Framing and the Big Idea | Reference projection, controllability, Big Idea, 3-minute story | Problem framing note (first pitch) |
| 5 | Designing the ideal and the value | Driving idea, TASCOI, conceptual model, value maps | Idealized design + value maps |
| 6 | Obstacles and evidence | Institutions/actors/results, hypothesis board, Test Cards | Obstacle chain + one real test |
| 7 | From design to project | Logframe, indicators, assumptions | One logframe |
| 8 | Telling the story for decision | Storyboard, visuals, narrative arc | Final decision pitch |

**Assessment idea:** the deliverables of the running case act as the portfolio (60%). The final pitch is graded with an SwD checklist plus a logframe quality rubric (30%). Learning cards serve as a reflective log (10%).

## 10. Open Points to Refine

1. **Name and language** of the line (ES/EN). Does "Dissolve · Design · Tell" work for your clients?
2. **Scope:** is this a consulting *method*, a *service offering* (with packaged deliverables and pricing), or both?
3. **Templates:** which canvases and worksheets to build first? Suggested order:
   - engagement brief;
   - actor profile (a VPD + DP hybrid);
   - controllability matrix;
   - TASCOI sheet;
   - hypothesis board;
   - logframe;
   - decision storyboard.
4. **Testing in the public sector:** define a menu of "public experiments" (pilots, co-design sessions, simulated decisions) to replace VPD's market tests.
5. **Case library:** which real or anonymized engagements to turn into teaching cases.
6. **Verification:** the SwD summary is based on general knowledge of the book. Check it against your copy before using it in class.
